How it works

Anki keeps the schedule. ReviewBar keeps it within reach.

ReviewBar drives Anki's own reviewer through the AnkiConnect add-on, on your machine. Every answer is recorded exactly as if you had done it in Anki.

  1. 01

    See what's due

    Open Anki as usual. ReviewBar reads the due count for the decks you chose and shows it in the menu bar.

  2. 02

    Click the notch

    A panel grows out of the notch with your next card, rendered with your note type's own CSS, images and audio. Space reveals the answer.

  3. 03

    Rate and move on

    Press 1 to 4. Interval previews come from Anki's scheduler. After a small batch, ReviewBar offers to stop, and Escape puts it away.

Built for the whole day

Small batches instead of one big backlog.

  • Real card rendering

    Your note-type CSS and scripts, images and audio, in the same DOM Anki's reviewer uses. Themes behave the way they do in Anki.

  • Soft sessions

    After a batch of cards, ReviewBar offers to stop rather than marching you through everything. Continue if you're on a roll.

  • Quiet reminders

    Haven't reviewed in a while and something is waiting? The notch peeks. A notification only when the notch can't be seen.

  • Keyboard first

    Space to reveal, 1 to 4 to rate, Escape to dismiss. Rating buttons carry the interval preview Anki would show.

  • Deck scope

    Pick the decks that belong in your day. The due count, the reminders and the review all follow the same choice.

  • Stays on your Mac

    ReviewBar talks only to AnkiConnect on localhost. No accounts, no telemetry, no other network requests. MIT licensed.

Free. Install in a minute.

Download the latest release and drag ReviewBar to Applications. Look for its icon in the menu bar.

Download for macOS

Latest release on GitHub · macOS 15 or later

Or from the Terminal

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gpressutto5/reviewbar-for-anki/main/scripts/install.sh | bash

Installs to /Applications with no root. Run it again to update. It's an 80-line script; worth a skim, like anything piped into a shell.

You'll need

  • macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon or Intel
  • Anki Desktop, running
  • The AnkiConnect add-on. In Anki: Tools → Add-ons → Get Add-ons…, paste 2055492159, restart.

First launch: "Apple cannot check it for malicious software"

ReviewBar isn't notarized, which needs a paid Apple developer membership. Dismiss the warning, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway next to ReviewBar. One-time step. The install script skips this.
